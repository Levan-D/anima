# Instrument ranges for the frequency chart

2026-11-01. What `chartData.ts` says each instrument's fundamentals reach, and where that comes from. All pitches are sounding, with C4 as middle C, computed from A4 = 440 Hz in equal temperament. `chartData.test.ts` holds every pitched instrument's numbers to the note names in its own note.

Tags: **[read]** the page or chart was read. **[snippet]** only a search result. **[pointer]** a secondary site, as a lead. **eye** is a reading off a chart, about a semitone either way. **tr.** is transposed from a written range by the source's own interval.

These are the sources I could reach. **VSL's Academy, Yamaha's guide and Grove were not read** (502, 403, not accessible), and no instrument maker's range was read for anything. Brooks and Absil are individual compilers' charts, not peer-reviewed, and they carry most of the woodwind and brass. Ranges also have no single truth: a player's top note varies, so a "practical" top and a "possible" top differ by a lot. **What's picked is the instrument's full usual range, for a general reader, and not what most parts use.**

**What's not from these sources:** kick, toms, snare, congas and cymbals have no pitch range to check, so they stay as read off IRN's chart. The same goes for harmonic ends, breath and overblow fringes, and the "body near" phrases in the notes: those are IRN's mixing advice, kept in our words and not re-checked. **Sound-word bands are subjective labels,** and the chart says so.

**Lowest confidence:** guitar, piano, organ (one compiler's chart) and timpani (no maker).
